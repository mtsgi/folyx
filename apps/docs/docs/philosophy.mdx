## Knowing where a value comes from [#facts]

A foldable screen can offer several ways to arrange an interface. Folyx helps by describing the information available to your app and making its source clear.

Some values come from observations: posture, for example, is reported by the selected Provider. Others are worked out from the regions, such as their layout and the gaps between them. A fallback region has a different purpose again: it gives an app a way to keep rendering when measurements aren't usable.

That distinction matters when a snapshot contains one region. It may be a browser measurement or a fallback covering the viewport. `source` tells you which one you're using, while `availability` describes whether the API could provide information. A single fallback region doesn't tell you how many physical screens a device has or whether it can fold.

An `unknown` value is useful too. It lets an app make its own choice with a clear view of what's known. Folyx keeps `unknown`, `null`, and fallback states visible instead of filling them with guessed dimensions, hinge angles, or posture.

## Leaving room for your interface [#geometry]

Folyx describes regions and their arrangement. Your app decides how to use them, whether that's a reading view, a list and its details, or something else. A gap describes space between regions; it may not represent a physical hinge or a measured area of occlusion.

The shared layout coordinate frame makes it possible to use Browser, Mock, and custom Providers with the same application code. The part of the page currently visible is a separate piece of information, so zooming or opening a keyboard doesn't silently change what a public coordinate means.

## Keeping the pieces small [#thin-core]

`getSnapshot()` reads state, and `subscribe()` follows it. Subscriptions look after the resources they need. The Vue adapter connects that state to component lifecycles, while FoldLab provides a place to record, compare, and explore measurements.

Keeping these responsibilities together in small pieces makes them easier to test. Core stays independent of frameworks and doesn't manipulate CSS, collect telemetry, store history, or maintain a device-specific lookup table. Immutable snapshots help make changes easy to follow and tests repeatable.

The state model also leaves room for new devices. A posture Folyx doesn't recognize can be represented as `unknown`, and a new arrangement can be represented as `complex` while its regions remain available to the app.
